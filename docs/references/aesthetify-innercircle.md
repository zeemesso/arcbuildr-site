## 3. Conversion mechanics

- **CTA:** "Get Instant Access", the same words 7 times (top bar, hero, after section 4, after section 7, pricing card, final, sticky bottom bar). Six go to `#pricing` (scroll), one to checkout. The scroll-then-buy two-step keeps the pitch in front of the buyer.
- **Qualification:** almost none. Hero says who it is not too early for ("no huge following"). No explicit "not for you".
- **Form fields:** none on page (checkout is external; **UNVERIFIED** what it asks).
- **Flow:** CTA, pricing anchor, checkout. Instant access, no call.
- **Scarcity/urgency:** "first 50 people only" (twice), the pop-up with "only 3 spots left", "open it this week". No countdown. The cap is unexplained (no way to verify).
- **Proof:** the weakest part. No named testimonials, results or logos. Only anonymous claims ("multi-millionaire founders", "9-figure personal brands") and the pop-up. The pop-up name changed between my two loads (Raj, then Aaron): it is generated, not real.
- **Risk reversal:** 14-day money-back in the pricing card. FAQ version is conditional (apply the systems, email first, extra guidance, then refund). The two statements conflict.
- **Price display:** shown early and repeatedly: $147 once, with a stacked value ($1,900 + $20/month) and a "vs $147/mo" comparison in the first line.
- **Disclaimer:** footer states results depend on effort.

## 4. Copy patterns

- Headline: `The system behind [desired identity] that [premium outcome]`. Identity-first ("be the brand they can't ignore").
- Short declarative contrast lines: "Your X is good. Your Y isn't." Pain list of four, each one sentence.
- The offer is named three ways: a product name, "OS" (operating system), and numbered "systems". Parts are numbered 01 to 07 so the buyer sees a path. Stack items each carry a dollar value.
- Outcome is vague (premium, attracts clients); process is concrete (SOPs, Figma boards, AI mentor).
- Quote (under 15 words): "Make your personal brand aesthetic, or you don't pay."

## 5. Mobile (VERIFIED, 390 px)

- A fixed bottom CTA bar ("Get Instant Access, $147 lifetime"), a sticky header CTA, a sticky section-heading strip, and a fixed pop-up. A video element is present in the hero. All sections stack.

## 6. Relevant to Arcbuildr

- "What happens after you join/book" as 4 short steps. For a high-ticket service this becomes "what happens after you apply" (call, audit, kickoff, first delivery).
- Name the work as numbered parts (01 to 05 for Arcbuildr's five deliverables) so the build reads as a path.
- Pre-handle the top 3 objections in the hero itself in one short line each ("no big following needed").
- Pain contrast pair: "your content is fine, your brand is not" maps well to a profile audit angle.
- Sticky bottom bar on phones; one CTA wording repeated.
- Future digital product: single page, one wording, a "what you get" stack, a 4-step onboarding, instant access, a clear refund line.
- Put a legal disclaimer in the footer (results depend on effort).

**Avoid:** fake or generated scarcity and join toasts; unnamed celebrity claims; contradictory guarantee text; value-stack dollar numbers we cannot defend. An anonymous-proof page will not carry a high-ticket sale.

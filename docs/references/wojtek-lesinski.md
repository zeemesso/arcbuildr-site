## 3. Conversion mechanics

- **CTA wording:** first-person, outcome verbs on the hub ("Build my media department") and short neutral verbs on the forms ("Get in touch", "Send"). One CTA per page. No repeated CTAs; pages are too short to need them.
- **Qualification:** by self-selection, through who-this-is-for bullets on `/consulting`; and by a work sample on `/editor`. Neither page lists a budget, minimum, or exclusion.
- **Form fields:** 4 (consulting) and 5 (editor). Both ask for a free-text need or a work sample, and one optional field (company).
- **Booking flow:** hub, route page, form, personal reply (consulting). Hub, application, review (editor). The advisory call is a separate Cal.com link kept off the consulting page.
- **Price:** none anywhere. The "Content Advisory" booking page may show one (**UNVERIFIED**, not opened).
- **Scarcity/urgency:** only "Applications open" and "being early puts you in it". No cap, no date.
- **Proof:** none on these pages. Authority is carried entirely by the person's public channels (video, 3 social links) and the first-person promise.
- **Risk reversal:** "I respond personally." No guarantee.

## 4. Copy patterns

- Very short: sentences of 5 to 10 words. Almost no adjectives.
- Positioning by contrast in a single line: "You work directly with me." (an anti-agency stance).
- Services are named as plain two-word nouns. No process is described and no outcome numbers are promised.
- Hub buttons are written from the visitor's side ("my media department"), form buttons from the neutral side.
- Quote (under 15 words): "You work directly with me."

## 5. Mobile

- The sites are one column and short. The header is fixed with a Back link. No sticky CTA. **VERIFIED** for `/consulting`; the hub and `/editor` were not rendered at 390 px (**UNVERIFIED**).

## 6. Relevant to Arcbuildr

- One-line anti-agency stance ("you work with the founders directly") fits a two-founder studio and needs no proof to say.
- Hub-and-route pattern for Instagram link-in-bio traffic: two big buttons by intent (service, digital product). Matches the future product.
- A who-it-is-for list of four bullets is a cheap, honest filter.
- Four-field contact form with a free-text "what do you need?" and a promise of a personal reply. Optional field for company.
- A separate booking link for a low-commitment paid call, kept off the main application page.
- "Applications open" status badge as the only scarcity device.

**Avoid:** a page with no proof at all (works only because he is already known); an undefined "once you qualify" note, which reads as vague; listing service names with no description of what is delivered.
